# Introduction
I must confess something: I think that I *like* Arial. Yes, *that* Arial, the
one that annoying people on my computer say is an awful ripoff of Helvetica.
Since I like Arial, but Arial is controlled by Microsoft and Monotype, I needed
an alternative---there are two that fit the bill here, Arimo and Ronzino, and it
is quite convenient that they both end in "-o."

Both of these fonts are made to replace Arial, but they go about it in different
ways: Arimo, designed by Steve Matteson, is supposed to be metrically
compatible with Arial, and shares a few common features, but ultimately is a
very different beast from Arial; Ronzino, designed by Luigi Gorlero and Pio
Mazzaferro at Collletttivo, by contrast is basically just an open
source revival of Arial with a few fixes. I like both of them fine, so choosing
between them is quite difficult.
(To be honest, both Arimo and Ronzino are stand-ins for my favorite of the
neutral-workhorse sans-serif fonts: Univers. My absolute *dream* is to have an
open source Univers revival with all the original widths and weights---same with
the original Frutiger font. They're wonderful fonts.)

Any of the regular styles are your standard workhorses. A lot of people like to
get mad about things like Swiss typography, but I think that it has a lot of
value. I'm not going to say that good design must inherently be invisible or
unobtrusive, because *good* design is contextual in the same way that a good
car is contextual. The Lancia Delta HF Integrale is an amazing car, but I doubt
anyone would want to take their kids to school in a rally car that can go from
0 to 100 km/h in two seconds on gravel and completely light on fire after a
crash; I also doubt that anyone would use a stock Toyota Corolla for Group B
rallying.

In this same way, something like Ronzino is a good workhorse. It's not designed
to be the most flashy or stand-out, it's designed to *work*. I know a lot of
twenty-somethings around my age are sick of workhorses and minimalism or
whatever the next buzzword is, but I think it's important to have something
that just works. A workhorse doesn't need to be boring, but it's important that
we don't let "functional" become erroneously synonymous with "boring."
Contemporary graphic design is filled with brightly colored things that,
visually, scream and shriek at you---I find that incredibly boring. *Sameness*,
rather than the technical aspects of some letterform, is boring.

`{\sffamily`{=latex} Ronzino satisfies a few big itches here: I want a font with square dots---for
lack of a better term---and angled ascenders on the **t**. Arimo, for as good
of a font as it is doesn't *quite* satisfy this itch. The first font that
*really* caught my eye was Computer Modern, and the second was Frutiger, which
unfortunately caused me to have the incredibly unrealistic expectation for
workhorse sans serif fonts---Adrian Frutiger was, for all intents and purposes,
an absolute genius in type design, and the way that Frutiger and Univers work
have a really excellent flow to them. This font comes reasonably close to
*Univers*, so I'm satisfied---I can't wait to see what the team at Collletttivo
manage to do with Ronzino. I just need to find out what the opentype feature
table is like, lol. `}`{=latex}
